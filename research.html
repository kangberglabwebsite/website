What we study

Research

We investigate how the human brain develops and changes across life, from the formation of neural stem-cell populations and organised tissue to the effects of inflammation, ageing and neurodegenerative disease. Our work combines human tissue with organoids, cerebroids and multicellular assembloids.

01

Neural stem cells & neurogenesis

We study neural stem cell identity and lineage progression during cortical and hippocampal development, including the developmental origins of neural stem cells that persist into adult life.

Explore neural stem cell research
02

Human brain organoids & 3D models

We develop human organoids, cerebroids and assembloids to model cortical and hippocampal development and to incorporate cell types such as microglia, vascular cells and choroid plexus tissue.

Explore our 3D brain models
03

Maternal inflammation & IL-17A

We investigate how inflammatory signals associated with maternal immune activation influence neural stem cells, neurogenesis and the organisation of the developing human cortex.

Explore maternal inflammation research
04

Brain ageing & neurodegeneration

We use human iPSC-derived neural cells, organoids and multicellular models to investigate cellular senescence, autophagy, APOE-dependent microglial function, choroid plexus biology and mechanisms relevant to Alzheimer's disease.

Explore ageing and neurodegeneration research

How we work

Human experimental neuroscience

Our research brings together developmental neurobiology, stem cell biology, neuroimmunology and human experimental models. We use human fetal tissue where appropriate alongside stem cell-derived systems that allow mechanisms to be tested experimentally.

Methods used across the group include high-resolution imaging and histology, pharmacological and cytokine perturbation, single-cell transcriptomics, spatial RNA analysis and functional measurements in 3D cultures.

These approaches let us connect molecular and cellular mechanisms with tissue-level organisation, from neural stem cell behaviour and cortical architecture to interactions among neural, immune, vascular and barrier cell types.

Human 3D neural tissue provides an experimental bridge between molecular mechanisms and tissue organisation.
